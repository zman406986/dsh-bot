# DD-171 — Send-acceptance ledger (durable digest of accepted sends)
- **Area:** A20 session & durability
- **Type:** missing-feature
- **Date:** 2026-09-23

## What the reference does
The reference's send pipeline keeps a prompt-acceptance ledger on disk. Before a send is dispatched, the pipeline computes a content digest (sha256 over the send inputs, including the prompt text and the optional rich-text, reply-target, fork, and attachment fields) and asks the ledger to admit the send, keyed on the account slot plus a client-supplied nonce, carrying the digest. The ledger answers one of three ways: it admits the send, it recognises the send as a duplicate, or it reports itself degraded. On a duplicate answer the pipeline treats the send as an idempotent no-op and returns without dispatching. If the in-flight attempt throws, the pipeline asks the ledger to drop the entry unless the send had already been accepted, so only genuinely accepted sends survive. The ledger is bounded — it holds at most 256 records — with a separate bounded window list for degraded intervals, and it can detect corruption and reset itself. So the acceptance record outlives the process, and a replayed send nonce after a restart is recognized rather than re-sent.

## What the current build does
The build has no on-disk send-acceptance ledger. Its send path has no journal at all: sends are direct, and there is no persistent record of what was accepted. The build does, however, carry a dedupe concern on the peer-messaging path. The peer inbox module under `src/core/` decides which peer messages are accepted and how they are delivered: it keeps a dedupe index alongside delivered flags and a per-session cap on undelivered messages (default 64), accepts synchronously without waiting for the result, is idempotent on an identical re-send, and refuses a re-send whose payload differs. That index lives in memory only — it is not persisted, and it is scoped to the peer envelope shape rather than to conversation sends. The peer tool in `src/core/tools.ts` is the surface that funnels a send into that inbox, with the acknowledgement reporting acceptance into the inbox and the delivery route.

## The deviation
The reference makes send acceptance durable: the record of an accepted send is written to disk, digest-keyed, capped, and survives restart, which buys idempotent replay of a send nonce across sessions. The build has no such durable record. The deviation is therefore not "the build lacks deduplication" — the build deduplicates peer messages, idempotently and with a cap — but "the build's deduplication of accepted sends is in-memory and non-durable." The gap is the persistence of the acceptance record, and the reference's SHA-256 content digest plus its 256-record cap are the two mechanisms the build would need if this were ever incorporated.

## Verdict: DO-NOT-INCORPORATE
ADR-0018 exception A4 is decisive and recorded: the enumerated exception "A4 — No send-journal semantics (no optimistic send / offline queue / flush-on-reconnect built by us); sends are direct" is restated for the wrapper era (2026). A durable digest ledger of accepted sends is precisely a send journal, and A4 excludes it. The build's in-memory peer inbox is the substitute the ruling's own framing implies: the dedupe concern is met where the build owns it, without persisting an acceptance record. No higher authority in the stack re-opens A4; the PRD carries no task requiring durable send acceptance, and no accepted ADR supersedes it. This is not an unruled question — it is ruled, and the ruling lands on DO-NOT-INCORPORATE.

This is recorded for completeness; A4 forecloses the work.
- **Constraints:** A4 (no send journal, sends direct) forbids optimistic send, offline queue, and flush-on-reconnect semantics; a durable digest ledger is a send journal and is likewise excluded. Any future proposal must first move A4 through the authority stack rather than route around it. The peer inbox's in-memory dedupe index and its 64-entry per-session cap are the build's existing, deliberate answer and should not be duplicated by a second persistence mechanism.
