# DD-196 — Self-summarization (agent compacts its own context)
- **Area:** A24 context management
- **Type:** missing-feature
- **Date:** 2026-09-23

## What the reference does
The reference ships a self-summarization module inside its agent package (`the reference tree's source/packages/agent/self-summary/`), dominated by one ~628-line handler. It fires on either of two recorded conditions, evaluated together in a single predicate: a turn count reaching 1000, or the consumed share of the model's context window reaching 90% (`constants.ts:1-3`, consumed at `self-summary.ts:75` and `self-summary.ts:91`). On firing it issues a summarization turn that is deliberately a no-tool-call turn — the summarization instruction tells the model to respond in prose only and to invoke nothing (`constants.ts:12`, and again in the provider-specific variants). Failure is handled by a retry ladder of at most 3 attempts (`self-summary-handler.ts:327-381`), and when retrying it degrades the *input* rather than the ambition: it preserves a fixed prefix and the trailing prompt message, then shrinks the middle of the message list — dropping tool-role middle messages when they dominate, and otherwise keeping only the later half of the middle (for a single middle message, the second half of its content) (`self-summary-handler.ts:259-307`). The whole design presupposes a long-lived agent whose own conversation can grow without bound.

## What the current build does
ABSENT. The build does not summarize, and it does not own its context window. Its workers are short-lived per-task leaf agents: the worker contract in `src/worker/prompt.ts` frames each worker as a single-job delegate, states that it spawns nothing and maintains no todo list, and names its only channel back as one final answer — the worker's life is its task. The dispatcher's own context is host-managed; `src/core/multitask.ts` contains no compaction, token accounting, or window ownership. The nearest thing the build has is `src/core/report-clamp.ts`: a hard character cut applied to a settled worker's report body before it enters a wake notice — keep the head, cut the tail, append a truncation marker that points at the full transcript.

## The deviation
The reference treats unbounded context growth as the agent's own problem to solve inside the conversation; the build removes the problem by design instead. A worker's context is the length of one task, and nothing in the build persists a conversation long enough to need compaction — so the trigger conditions have no referent here: both a turn-count threshold and a window-fraction threshold presuppose a long-lived conversation with a large window, and the build's leaf workers have neither. The one transferable design idea worth naming is the retry ladder's degradation strategy: when summarization keeps failing, shrink the input rather than the goal, preserving the stable prefix and the current instruction while reducing or halving the middle. The build's report clamp is a cruder version of that idea — it also trades fidelity for fit under a limit — but it cuts the tail of a finished artifact rather than reducing a live input, and no retry ladder sits behind it.

## Verdict: DO-NOT-INCORPORATE
No recorded ruling covers self-summarization as such — DD-192 and DD-195, both DO-NOT-INCORPORATE, decide the surrounding summarization family, and this entry does not re-open them. The build's own design settles it: workers are deliberately short-lived per-task leaf contracts (`src/worker/prompt.ts`), so there is no long-lived context for a trigger to fire on, and the dispatcher's window is the host's to manage, not ours. Incorporating a self-summarizer would mean inventing a conversation lifetime the build does not have. The degradation idea is recorded above for reference only; the report clamp already covers the fit-under-a-bound need in the single place the build has that need.
