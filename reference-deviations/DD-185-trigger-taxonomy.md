# DD-185 — Trigger taxonomy: cron, interval, and third-party integrations
- **Area:** A22 automation
- **Type:** deliberate-divergence
- **Date:** 2026-09-23

## What the reference does
The reference ships a full trigger taxonomy for its automations subsystem, split across a shared layer and a host layer. A shared schedule module parses calendar expressions (with shorthand aliases for common cadences), a separate interval-style "every N units" form, and a timezone convention — a zone prefix on the expression, resolved through locale-aware zone formatting — and computes the next run instant from any of them (`"reference material-NOT LICENSE SAFE DONT COPY-grok bot/source/shared/automation-schedule.ts"` lines 2–4, 9, 11, 13). A second shared module defines the trigger union: a schedule trigger plus event triggers for six third-party services — a chat platform, a code host (a catalog of fourteen event kinds), a team chat platform, an issue tracker, an error monitor, and an incident platform — and a composite trigger carrying several listener members at once, bounded at eight listeners, with a parallel bound of eight on reaction-based listeners (`"reference material-NOT LICENSE SAFE DONT COPY-grok bot/source/shared/automations.ts"` lines 1–2). The trigger contract lives host-side in its own module beside the automation record, store, and status-reminder modules (`"reference material-NOT LICENSE SAFE DONT COPY-grok bot/source/host/automations/automation-trigger.ts"`, `automation.ts`, `automation-store.ts`), with delivery running through an extensions-side trigger hub and cloud trigger/consumer path (`"reference material-NOT LICENSE SAFE DONT COPY-grok bot/source/host/extensions/automations/"`). The frontend mirrors the taxonomy with a schedule module, a trigger schema, a schedule editor, and a trigger draft controller (`"reference material-NOT LICENSE SAFE DONT COPY-grok bot/frontend/src/recovered/features/automations/routines/"`).

## What the current build does
The build's wake bus declares six wake sources, only two of which are non-conversational (`src/core/wake-bus.ts` lines 14–20, 23–30). The "timer" kind is not a scheduler: it is the redelivery wake — a wake with no payload, built for the delivery-retry pass (DD-172), held as a coalescing flag, re-armed on reclaim, and emitted at turn end rather than on any wall clock (`src/core/wake-bus.ts` lines 205–207, 322–335, 353–359, 543–552; `src/core/multitask.ts` lines 791–805). The "external-event" kind is declared and then never produced or consumed anywhere under `src/` or `client/` — a placeholder for future use, nothing more. There is no calendar parser, no interval syntax, no timezone handling, and no way to connect any third-party service. ADR-0018 C14 rules out the routines/automations subsystem (DD-183) and C15 rules the single wake kind (DD-174). The study's capability-gaps register records this as Gap 4, "no time-based or external triggers (PRD: user/worker/peer only)", recommending that the wake bus be made source-agnostic by accepting a timer and an external-event source (`docs/research/prd-vs-reference-capability-gaps.md` lines 42–48) — a recommendation the bus vocabulary took up while the scheduling and integration layers stayed unbuilt.

## The deviation
The reference treats a trigger as a first-class typed taxonomy spanning schedules (calendar, interval, zoned) and six external event feeds, with a bounded composite form and its own dedicated host module. The build has the opposite arrangement: a wake vocabulary that accepts any source by name but only handles conversational ones in practice — its only non-conversational wake is an internal redelivery nudge, and no external event can reach it. What is missing is not a parameter but a whole subsystem: scheduling, trigger typing, and integration adapters are all absent.

## Verdict: DO-NOT-INCORPORATE
ADR-0018 C14 rules out the routines/automations subsystem outright, and C15 rules the single wake kind — only dispatched workers flow through the wake bus, the ledger, and the running-tasks view, with no background one-shot commands and no remote/cloud jobs as wake kinds. Together those two exceptions cover the whole of this deviation: the taxonomy, its schedules, and its integrations all presuppose the subsystem C14 excludes and the additional wake kinds C15 excludes. The capability-gaps register's Gap 4 recommendation is already satisfied in the narrow sense that the bus declares the extra sources, so nothing here is left unruled — it is ruled and declined. The unused external-event kind is an observation about the build's own shape, not a recommendation to populate it; the sibling rulings are DD-183 (routines subsystem) and DD-174 (wake taxonomy), with the proactive-automation stance in DD-184.

## Incorporation notes
- **Where it would live:** nowhere under current rulings. Were the ruling ever reversed, the honest home would be a new scheduler/trigger module under `src/core/` feeding the wake bus through the already-declared external-event source, with integration adapters under `src/adapter/` — a subsystem addition, not a patch to an existing file.
- **Constraints:** C14 and C15 both bind. A calendar-expression parser plus six authenticated integration adapters would also bring in credential storage, outbound network access, and retry-on-delivery behaviour the build deliberately does not carry, and the reference's listener limit and per-platform event catalogs are its own product choices rather than transferable requirements.
- **License note:** ADR-0018 B9 — re-derive independently; no verbatim copying from the reference tree.
- **Effort/risk:** L — a scheduler plus six integration adapters is a subsystem with its own credential handling, outbound network access, and retry behaviour; the risk is scope and the direct C14/C15 conflict, not implementation difficulty.
